# Making money from Studylark

Read this with a parent. Some of it needs them, and the parts that do are
marked.

---

## 1. The honest starting point

Most free tools make nothing. The ones that make something almost always follow
the same order:

> **useful thing → people actually using it → money**

You cannot skip the middle step, and the middle step is the hard one. Studylark
is a genuinely good product on day one — the auto-card-making is a real
advantage over Quizlet and Anki, not a gimmick. But nobody knows it exists yet,
and a great tool with twelve users earns twelve users' worth of money.

So: **the work ahead is distribution, not code.** Ignore anyone who tells you
otherwise.

**Realistic numbers**, so nothing is a surprise:

| Monthly active users | Realistic monthly income |
|---|---|
| 50 | £0 |
| 500 | £0–10 in tips |
| 5,000 | £20–120 (tips + a small paid product) |
| 50,000 | £300–2,000, and now it's a real thing |

Getting to 500 real users is a genuine achievement that most adult side projects
never reach. Treat that as the target, not the £2,000 row.

## 2. What you can do yourself vs what needs a parent

You're 12. That's not a problem for building — it's a problem for *accounts*,
because money platforms are contracts and under-18s generally can't hold one.

**You can do all of this on your own:**

- build and improve the app
- put it online with GitHub Pages (free)
- show it to your class, your teachers, your friends
- ask teachers to link it from a school page
- write the help pages, make the decks, answer feedback

**These need a parent's name on the account** (they own it, you run it):

| Thing | Typical minimum age |
|---|---|
| Stripe / PayPal business | 18 |
| Ko-fi / Buy Me a Coffee payouts | 18 |
| Gumroad | 18 |
| GitHub Sponsors | 18 |
| A bank account that receives it | 16–18 |

Also worth checking together: most social platforms (TikTok, Reddit, Instagram,
X, Discord) set 13 as the minimum age, so posting there is something a parent
either does or supervises for now. Rules change — check the current terms rather
than trusting this table.

**None of this is a blocker.** A parent opening a Ko-fi account takes about five
minutes. Frame it that way when you ask.

## 3. Four ways this could make money, best first

### A. Tips — start here
Add a Ko-fi or Buy Me a Coffee link (parent's account). Open
`index.html`, find `var SUPPORT_URL = "";` near the top of the script, and put
the URL between the quotes. The footer link appears automatically.

- **Effort:** 10 minutes
- **Realistic:** roughly 1 in 500 regular users tips, usually £3
- **Why first:** costs nothing, risks nothing, and asks nothing of people who
  can't pay

### B. Deck packs — the most realistic real income
Sell carefully-made revision decks: *GCSE Biology Paper 1*, *Spanish verbs*,
*Year 8 chemical equations*. £2–4 each on Gumroad (parent's account). The app
stays free forever; the packs are a shortcut people pay for.

- **Effort:** high — a good 200-card deck takes several hours
- **Realistic:** 1–3% of engaged visitors buy; 2,000 visitors ≈ £60–200
- **Critical:** write the decks yourself from your own understanding. Copying a
  textbook, a revision guide, or another site's deck is copyright infringement,
  and "I'm 12" is not a defence. Facts are free; someone else's *wording* is not.

### C. Schools and tutors
A tutor with 20 students is worth more than 500 random visitors. Offer a small
bundle — decks for a specific syllabus, plus help setting it up — for £20–50.

- **Effort:** medium, mostly emails a parent should send or co-sign
- **Realistic:** slow, but the best money per hour if even one says yes

### D. Sponsorship — later, not now
Once you have real traffic (5,000+ a month), education companies sometimes pay
for a small "supported by" line. Don't chase this until the traffic exists.

### Not recommended: ads
Ad networks on a site aimed at under-13s drag you into COPPA and the UK
Children's Code — strict laws about children's data. Ads also make the app worse
and contradict the privacy promise that makes it good. Skip them.

## 4. The first 30 days

**Week 1 — ship it**
1. Turn on GitHub Pages (**Settings → Pages → Source → GitHub Actions**).
2. Rename the repository to something you'd say out loud: `studylark`. The URL
   becomes your product's address, and the current one undersells it.
3. Use it yourself for a real test at school. If you don't use it, nobody will.

**Week 2 — ten real users**
4. Show five classmates. Watch them use it *without helping*. Every place they
   hesitate is a bug, even if the code is fine.
5. Fix the top three things they trip over.
6. Make one genuinely excellent deck for a test your year group has coming up
   and share the link. The link *is* the product demo.

**Week 3 — a teacher**
7. Show the teacher whose subject the deck covers. Teachers talk to other
   teachers, and one saying "use this" beats a thousand random clicks.
8. Ask a parent to post it once to r/GetStudying or a study Discord — honestly,
   as "my kid built this, it's free, no account."

**Week 4 — turn it on**
9. Parent opens Ko-fi; you add the URL.
10. Start deck pack #1 only if week 2–3 produced people who came *back*. Repeat
    users are the signal worth trusting.

## 5. Rules that keep you out of trouble

- **Never collect personal data.** Right now Studylark collects zero, which is
  its best legal feature. Adding accounts or email sign-ups would drop children's
  privacy law on your head. Don't.
- **Write your own content.** See the warning in section B.
- **Don't claim it's a study cure.** "Spaced repetition helps you remember for
  longer" is true. "Get an A*" is not, and overclaiming is how you lose teachers.
- **Every payment account is your parent's**, in their name, with their bank
  details. Tax on small amounts is usually nothing, but it's their call and
  their paperwork.
- **Keep the app free.** The free app is what earns the audience; the packs are
  what earn the money. Paywalling the app kills both.

---

## 6. How to tell if it's working

Ignore total visits. Watch these:

1. **Do people come back the next day?** Spaced repetition only works if they
   return, so returning users are the whole game.
2. **Does anyone share a deck link without you asking?** That's the growth
   engine working by itself.
3. **Does anyone say "I used it for my test"?** That sentence is worth more than
   any number.

If all three are happening and you still make no money, the product is fine and
only the asking is missing. If none are happening, more marketing won't help —
go back to the app.

## 7. What was actually promised here

To be straight with you: nobody can guarantee this makes money, and anyone who
says otherwise is selling something. What you have is a real, working, genuinely
useful product that costs £0 to run and is better than the paid competition at
one specific thing. That's a much better starting position than most people get.

The rest is showing up.
