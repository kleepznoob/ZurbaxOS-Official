    Zurbax

    A desktop operating system of liquid glass and moving pixels. Tiled, themed, and fast, with Steam and every driver already on it.

    Get Zurbax See it in action

    One image for every PC made in the last twelve years · Free · No account, no telemetry

    An operating system, made whole.

    Zurbax is written line by line in C: its graphics, its window system and desktop, its toolkit, its apps, its network and sound, its installer and its updates, on Ember, a Linux kernel with its own patches. Nothing borrowed is bolted on, so the whole thing looks like one thing and starts in seconds.

    Then it opens the door to everything else: Steam and Proton for your games, all of Flathub for apps, Bridge for Windows programs, and GNOME if you want it.

    • One image. Every graphics card, every Wi-Fi chip, about five thousand drivers, loaded only for what's there.
    • Yours. No account to make, no telemetry, nothing listening on the network.
    • Kept safe. Signed security updates every two weeks, until October 2028.
    • Quick. From the firmware's logo to your desktop with not a line of text in between.

    See it in action

    Real screens, straight from Zurbax.

    the desktop

    Hive. Everything a keystroke away.

    Windows share the screen side by side and nine workspaces slide across. The bar floats as three islands of glass, and a bead of your accent flows from workspace to workspace. The window in focus wears a ring of the theme's two colours and a soft glow that follows you.

    Its window buttons are drawn in pixels: a tile of colour builds up behind each one block by block as you point at it. Put a window away and it waits in a tray in the bar.

    Two themes. This page wears them too.

    Carbon, black and orange, and Midnight, deep blue and violet. Each colours the rings, the glass and every app, and brings its own pixel-art wallpaper, painted by the system itself. Pick one and watch.

      a window, arriving

      the pixel style

      It moves.

      The wallpapers are alive, on their own grid of big pixels: stars twinkle and now and then one shoots across, lines run down Carbon's grid towards you, and light glitters on the river under Midnight's city.

      Windows build up block by block as they open and fall apart the same way as they close. Maximize one, or send it to the tray, and it passes through a mosaic on the way. The pointer, the clock and the workspace numbers are drawn in pixels too.

      All of it costs about a quarter of one percent of a processor, and one switch in Settings turns it off.

      the first start

      Even the setup is a show.

      The name builds itself in big pixels, block by block, over the theme's own picture while stars twinkle behind it. The steps are keycaps, the buttons are blocks you press down, and the tick at the end draws itself and throws sparks.

      Pick Carbon or Midnight on the way and the whole setup changes colour on the spot. The installer is the same: it shows what's coming in pixels that dissolve, and a live log of every step.

        Start from it
